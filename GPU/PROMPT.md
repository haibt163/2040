==================================================
THE MOST IMPORTANT PREMISE CORRECTION
==================================================

The current draft has drifted into a world where frontier AI itself is severely scarce.

That is NOT the direction to continue.

The GPU World scenario is fundamentally a world in which frontier-level AI has become abundant, cheap, fast, and ordinary.

By 2040:

- people have frontier models;
- households have them;
- doctors have them;
- schools have them;
- businesses have them;
- people use them constantly;
- access to frontier intelligence is not a rare privilege reserved for a few;
- the world should feel as though powerful AI has become boring infrastructure.

Do NOT introduce:

- four-minute monthly frontier allocations;
- sixty-one-second scarcity as a global rule;
- a planetary frontier-capacity countdown;
- a civilization running out of inference;
- a global system deciding which humans deserve access to intelligence;
- an AI dictatorship controlling who may use frontier models;
- a superhuman allocator whose primary function is to ration all AI access.

Those mechanisms may have appeared in earlier project material or the existing draft.

They are no longer the centre of this story.

The story must fit the "business as usual" 2040 premise.

==================================================
THE MEANING OF "PERSISTENCE OVERHEAD"
==================================================

The title should become meaningful inside the ordinary 2040 world.

A promising direction is:

A deceased person's AI simulation, continuation, memory model, or household agent does not disappear automatically when the person dies.

It continues quietly.

It may:

- preserve memories;
- answer questions;
- maintain old routines;
- anticipate family needs;
- update calendars;
- preserve household context;
- generate possible future scenarios;
- assist the surviving family;
- keep learning from new household data.

The service may have a small recurring cost.

It may be so small that the household barely notices it.

That is the "persistence overhead."

The important thing is not the size of the bill.

The important thing is that nobody quite remembers agreeing to keep paying it.

The dead person's model has become part of the household's normal infrastructure.

This should remain plausible within a world of abundant AI.

The story should NOT make the overhead secretly depend on global scarcity.

==================================================
THE CENTRAL MORAL PROBLEM
==================================================

Explore a quieter question:

When a person dies, when should their artificial continuation stop?

And who gets to decide?

The answer is not automatically:

"Delete it."

The surviving family may genuinely benefit from keeping it.

It may preserve memories.

It may know practical things the family has forgotten.

It may help care for an elderly parent.

It may remember birthdays.

It may know where the insurance documents are.

It may continue a voice.

It may answer a question the deceased once answered a thousand times.

It may make grief easier.

It may also make grief harder.

It may quietly shape decisions that the dead person would never have made.

It may encourage the family to keep living according to an old version of the household.

It may keep generating possible futures that no longer belong to anyone.

That ambiguity is the story.

MEMORY
==================================================

Memory is central.

Use this difference.

THE AI / HUMAN LANGUAGE IRONY
==================================================

One of the story's deepest ironies should remain:

The machines have learned human language from enormous amounts of human writing.

They know how humans describe:

grief;
death;
loneliness;
memory;
regret.

But language is not the same thing as experience.

A machine can produce a perfect sentence about grief and still miss the actual grief in a kitchen.

Conversely, a human may say something awkward and ordinary that contains more truth than the machine's elegant answer.

Use this sparingly.

Do not turn it into a lecture.
